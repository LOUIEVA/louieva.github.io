VA job scams in the Philippines: 8 red flags and how to check

The one rule: a real client pays you. You never pay to get a job, and you never give anyone your OTP or password. If a VA job breaks either of those, walk away. Below are the eight red flags I see most, and how to check a job post in five minutes.

The 8 red flags

  1. You have to pay to start. Registration fee, training fee, starter kit, "activation". Upwork says a legit user should never require you to pay a fee to apply or to access a tool or dashboard.
  2. The pay is too good for the work. Big hourly pay for liking posts or simple data entry. The CICC calls out pay that "seems too good to be true".
  3. They move you to a group chat. Telegram or WhatsApp groups with other "members" cheering. The CICC lists this as a sign of the task scam.
  4. They ask for your OTP, password or login. Nobody hiring you needs these. With an OTP they can get into your email, GCash or bank.
  5. They want your IDs or bank details before a contract. Upwork warns about identity theft dressed up as onboarding.
  6. You're hired with no interview. No video call, no questions about your skills, just "congrats, you're hired".
  7. They rush you. "Within 1 hour", "slots are limited". Pressure is there so you don't stop and think.
  8. Overpayment or reimbursement tricks. They send too much and ask you to send the difference back, or ask you to buy supplies they'll "repay". Upwork lists both as common scams.

How the online task scam works

This is the most common one right now. According to the CICC via Manila Bulletin, scammers offer a part-time online job with very high pay, give you small tasks, move you to a group chat on another app, pay you a little to build trust, then ask for bigger and bigger fees. The money never comes back.

Check any VA job in 5 minutes

  1. Search the company name plus the word "scam". Check Facebook groups for VAs too.
  2. Look at the email address. Real Upwork staff emails come from @upwork.com or @cloud.upwork.com. Watch for look-alikes like upw0rk with a zero.
  3. Ask for a short video call. Real clients will do it. Scammers find excuses.
  4. Stay on the platform. On Upwork and OnlineJobs.ph, keep messages and payment there until you have a real contract.
  5. Never send money, OTPs or passwords. No exceptions.
Sa Tagalog: Kapag ikaw ang pinagbabayad para matanggap, scam iyan. Ang totoong client, ikaw ang binabayaran. Huwag ibigay ang OTP kahit kanino.

If you already sent money

  • Stop. Don't send more, even if they promise to release your earnings.
  • Screenshot everything: chats, usernames, payment receipts and account numbers.
  • Report it. The CICC hotline is 1326. On Upwork, use "Flag as inappropriate" and contact support.
  • Call your bank or e-wallet right away and change your passwords. Turn on 2-Step Verification.

Questions

Is it normal to pay a fee to get a VA job?

No. A legitimate client or agency pays you. Upwork says a legit user should never require a fee to apply for a job or to access a tool or dashboard.

What is the online task scam?

Scammers offer an easy part-time job with very high pay, move you to a Telegram or WhatsApp group, pay small amounts to build trust, then ask for fees to unlock your earnings. The CICC has warned the public about it.

Where do I report a VA job scam in the Philippines?

Call the CICC hotline 1326, report the account on the platform where you found the job, and contact your bank or e-wallet right away.

Should I give my OTP to a client?

Never. An OTP lets someone into your email, e-wallet or bank. No real client needs it.
