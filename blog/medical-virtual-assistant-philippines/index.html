Medical virtual assistant in the Philippines: the job, the pay and how to start

A medical virtual assistant (also called a healthcare VA) is the remote admin staff of a clinic, usually in the US. You answer patient calls, book appointments, check insurance, chase claims and keep the EMR up to date. You don't diagnose or treat anyone. Below is what clinics ask for in 2026 job posts, what they pay, and the easiest way in.

What a medical VA does

Wing Assistant groups the work into five areas. In the job posts I read for this guide, the same tasks come up again and again.

  • Scheduling and patient calls: booking, rescheduling, confirmations and reminders.
  • Insurance verification: checking that a plan is active, plus copays, deductibles and prior authorizations.
  • Billing and claims: entering charges, posting payments and following up on unpaid or denied claims.
  • EMR and documents: updating patient records and uploading labs, notes and forms.
  • Referrals: processing referrals and booking the follow-up.

What clinics ask for in 2026

From a medical receptionist VA post, a patient support VA post and a Quezon City medical VA agency post:

  • Healthcare or medical office experience, often at least 1 year. One agency asks for 1 to 2 years in a medical-related background.
  • NBI or police clearance. Both Workable posts mark it as mandatory.
  • US hours. One post is 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM EDT, another says to be open to night shifts.
  • Equipment: a noise-cancelling headset, a webcam and a quiet room. Internet asks range from a minimum of 10 Mbps to 100 Mbps contracted fiber.
  • EMR names you'll see: Epic, Cerner, athenahealth, NextGen, Modernizing Medicine and TheraOffice.

How much medical VAs earn

  • $5 to $6 an hour for full-time patient support and medical receptionist roles in both Workable posts.
  • $800 a month starting rate at one Quezon City agency, with HMO coverage and paid time off.
  • ₱20,000 to ₱25,000 a month for some entry-level medical VA roles, according to Bossjob.

These are examples from live posts, not fixed rates. For general VA pay, see VA rates in the Philippines (2026).

The easiest way in

  1. Use any healthcare background you have. Nursing, caregiving, pharmacy or clinic front desk all count. Put it at the top of your resume.
  2. Learn HIPAA basics before you apply. Many agencies give HIPAA training once you're hired, but you'll get asked about it in the interview. Start with HIPAA for medical VAs.
  3. Aim for patient support or scheduling first. It's the most common entry role, and it teaches you the EMR and the clinic's workflow.
  4. Prepare for the interview with the questions in medical VA interview questions.

Questions

Can I become a medical VA without a medical degree?

Yes. Many roles are admin work like scheduling and patient calls. A healthcare background helps a lot, and many posts ask for at least a year of healthcare or medical office experience, so start with patient support roles if you're new.

How much does a medical VA earn in the Philippines?

2026 job posts show $5 to $6 an hour for full-time patient support roles and a $800 a month starting rate at one agency. Bossjob lists some entry-level medical VA roles at ₱20,000 to ₱25,000 a month.

What does a medical VA do every day?

Answer patient calls, book and confirm appointments, verify insurance, update the EMR and follow up on claims and referrals.

Is a healthcare VA the same as a medical VA?

Mostly yes. Job posts use both names for remote admin work for clinics and doctors: patient calls, scheduling, insurance checks, billing support and EMR updates.

What does a medical billing VA do?

A medical billing VA enters charges, submits claims, posts payments and follows up on unpaid or denied claims. It usually needs some training on CPT and ICD-10 codes, so many people start in scheduling or patient support first.

Do medical VAs work night shift?

Usually yes, because most clients are US clinics. One 2026 post runs 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM EDT, which is evening to early morning in the Philippines.
